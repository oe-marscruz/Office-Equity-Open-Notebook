### Format-specific acceptance

**Cross-format:** Use meaningful structure, appropriate text alternatives, descriptive links, understandable instructions, and equivalent access to information. Avoid using color alone. Measure actual foreground/background pairings. Normal text generally needs 4.5:1 contrast; large text 3:1, using the actual WCAG size definition and applicable exceptions. Design preferences such as a 24-point slide body are not WCAG rules. Sources: [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/), [contrast guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html).

**PPTX/Slides:** Unique descriptive slide titles; logical reading order; editable text; accessible layouts; appropriate visual alternatives; clear links; complete notes; captions/descriptions for media; authoring-app checker and screen-reader sampling. Verify the exported PPTX as well as the source editor. Source: [Microsoft presentation guidance](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/accessibility/powerpoint/make-your-powerpoint-presentations-accessible-to-people-with-disabilities).

**DOCX/Docs:** Real headings and lists; logical navigation; simple tables with headers; descriptive hyperlinks; appropriate alt text; document language; accessible forms if any. Use real page/section structure instead of text boxes as the main reading flow. Source: [Microsoft Word guidance](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/accessibility/word/create-more-accessible-word-documents).

**PDF:** Searchable text; correct semantic tags and reading order; title/language; bookmarks for longer documents; usable link/form annotations; properly represented tables and figures; usable magnification/reflow where applicable. Inspect the actual exported PDF with an available checker and assistive technology; do not print to a flattened image PDF. Track the chosen PDF/UA target separately if required. WCAG2ICT informs application to non-web documents; PDF/UA and WCAG are distinct. Source: [WCAG2ICT](https://www.w3.org/TR/wcag2ict/).

**HTML:** Semantic headings/landmarks; native keyboard-operable controls; visible, unobscured focus; labeled inputs and accessible feedback; text alternatives; zoom/reflow; no unnecessary motion or timed barriers. Test at 200% text resize and a 320 CSS-pixel-equivalent viewport for relevant reflow requirements. Evaluate AA target sizing or documented exceptions. Run an available automated checker and manual keyboard/screen-reader tests. Sources: [reflow](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/reflow.html), [target size](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html).

**Flyers/graphics:** Retain editable text and a complete HTML/text counterpart; pair QR codes with readable usable URLs; provide meaningful descriptions and equivalent structured information for diagrams. Decorative imagery may be marked decorative in its delivery context.

**Email signatures:** Provide accessible HTML and plain text; keep names/contact details as real text; verify links, contrast, images-disabled behavior, and rendering in relevant email clients. Document institutional brand approval separately.

**Zoom backgrounds:** Treat them as decorative support, with clear speaker safe areas, restrained visual noise, and appropriate aspect ratio. Essential instructions/contact details must exist outside the image in an accessible channel. Do not assert that a bitmap background alone conforms to WCAG.

**Audio/video if produced:** Supply accurate reviewed transcripts, synchronized captions for video, descriptions of essential visual content as required, and accessible playback. Narration does not replace an accessible document.
